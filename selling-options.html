Choosing How to Sell

Consignment vs. Facebook Marketplace and Other Ways to Sell

The best route depends on what you value most: control, speed, privacy, convenience, or the amount of work you are willing to manage. Here's a candid look at the tradeoffs.

Compare the options How Changing Places works

The Core Difference

Do You Want to Run the Sale or Hand Off the Buyer-Facing Work?

A direct sale gives you more control over price and timing. Consignment reduces the tasks that can make a furniture sale feel like another job.

With a self-managed listing

  • Clean, stage, measure, and photograph the item.
  • Write and refresh the listing.
  • Answer messages, screen buyers, and negotiate.
  • Manage no-shows, rescheduling, payment, and pickup.
  • Decide whether and when strangers may enter your home.

With Changing Places

  • Send photos for approval before making a trip.
  • Transport approved pieces to the store.
  • Reach shoppers — including destination visitors — through professional pricing and presentation in a staffed showroom.
  • Avoid repeated buyer messages and one-on-one negotiations.
  • Receive 45% of the final sale price if the item sells.

The Hidden Work

What a DIY Furniture Listing Actually Requires

Posting can take minutes. Managing the sale often takes much longer.

Photography and listing upkeep

Useful listings need clear angles, dimensions, condition details, an accurate description, and a price. If interest slows, you may revise, repost, or add more information.

Messages, no-shows, and negotiation

You may answer the same questions repeatedly, sort genuine buyers from low-effort inquiries, respond to offers, and reopen the conversation after someone fails to arrive.

Safety and pickup coordination

You decide what contact information to share, where to meet, who may enter the home, how payment is handled, and whether the buyer has enough people and the right vehicle.

A Fair Choice

When Another Selling Method May Be the Better Fit

Changing Places works well as a focused channel for appropriate furnishings and can sit alongside faster or more specialized routes when a household has mixed needs.

Choose Marketplace or Craigslist when…

You want to set your own price, keep the direct-sale proceeds, respond quickly, and manage pickup yourself. It can work well for a practical item priced to move.

Choose a tag or moving sale when…

You have many smaller or lower-priced household items, can host shoppers, and want a single event before donating or disposing of leftovers.

Choose an estate sale when…

A large share of a household needs to be inventoried, staged, sold, and cleared together. Read the provider's scope, fees, minimums, and post-sale plan carefully.

Choose consignment when…

Your approved furniture can wait for showroom exposure and you prefer professional pricing and presentation without managing individual shoppers yourself.

Using more than one route is normal

A move or estate may send select furniture to consignment, collectibles to a specialist, everyday goods to a sale, and remaining items to donation. The right plan can be a mix rather than a single all-or-nothing decision. See the guides for moving and downsizing or estate furniture.
