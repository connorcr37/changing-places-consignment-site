Furniture for College Apartments

Moving from a dorm or family home into an apartment is a chance to choose pieces with more character and useful life than a one-semester setup. Shop with a shared plan, accurate measurements, and flexibility about what is available that day.

Decide Before Move-In Day

Separate Shared Needs From Personal Rooms

A quick roommate plan can prevent duplicate purchases, awkward reimbursements, and furniture that no one can take at the end of the lease.

Students and roommates

List who is bringing what, who owns each shared purchase, and what happens when the lease ends. Assign one person to confirm measurements and another to coordinate the vehicle.

Parents helping furnish

Agree on a total furnishing budget and the pieces that matter most for comfort, study, storage, and shared meals. Shop together when possible so fit, condition, and transportation decisions happen in real time.

Graduate and professional students

Look beyond one lease. A well-constructed desk, table, chair, lamp, or storage piece may suit a future apartment or first post-school home if its size stays flexible.

Apartment-Friendly Thinking

Shop for Scale, Flexibility, and the Next Move

Because the selection changes daily, use these filters to recognize a good apartment fit whenever it appears.

  • Proportions that leave clear walkways in smaller rooms
  • Pieces that can serve more than one purpose
  • Construction and condition that you have inspected in person
  • Shapes that can move through apartment doors, halls, and stairs
  • Sizes likely to work in more than one future layout
  • A look that reflects the students who live there

Better quality is something to inspect, not assume

Secondhand shopping lets you compare materials, joinery, weight, finish, and wear in person. That can reveal sturdier options than some disposable furniture, but age or appearance alone does not guarantee quality.

Check stability, drawers, hardware, upholstery condition, odors, and surfaces before purchasing.

A Smarter Furnishing Budget

Spend According to Daily Use

A budget works better when it reflects which pieces will be used every day and which can wait.

Set the total

Include furniture, transportation, cleaning supplies, small household basics, and any building move-in costs in one realistic number.

Fund the essentials

Prioritize seating, a place to eat or study, lighting, and useful storage based on the apartment and what residents already own.

Leave room for finds

Keep part of the budget unassigned so a distinctive rug, mirror, art piece, or table can add individuality when the right option appears.

Document ownership

Record who paid for shared pieces. A simple note now makes the end of the lease easier for students and parents alike.

Move-In Logistics

Measure the Apartment and the Vehicle

Bring building measurements

Measure the room, doorway, hall, stairwell, elevator opening and interior, plus any tight turns. Confirm building rules and move-in times before shopping.

Confirm the pickup plan

Measure the vehicle's cargo opening and usable interior. Changing Places does not provide delivery, though staff can typically help load a suitable vehicle.

Ask about short storage

Purchased items can usually remain at the store for 7–10 days, subject to confirmation before purchase. A trusted full-service mover may also be recommended.

Come ready to decide. Merchandise is generally first-come, first-served. Measurements, roommate decisions, and a pickup plan make it easier to act when the right piece appears, since the store usually cannot hold an item for later purchase or a future markdown.
