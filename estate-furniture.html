A Clear Sequence

A Simple Path From Home to Showroom

Once the family knows what it wants to keep and who can make decisions, getting started is straightforward.

Confirm authority and family decisions

Before offering an item, confirm that the person acting for the estate is authorized to do so and that family distributions or holds have been addressed. We don't determine ownership or legal authority.

Separate keepsakes and specialist items

Remove personal documents, photographs, heirlooms, and items needing formal appraisal or specialty expertise. Consignment evaluation is based on resale fit, not estate or tax valuation.

Text Steph to get started

For a few pieces, send clear photos and note any wear. For a whole house or large group, just describe the scope first—we may be able to arrange a quick look instead of asking for hundreds of photos.

Hear what may work

Steph will let you know which pieces may suit our current space and shopper demand. Other belongings can continue through the estate's sale, donation, or clean-out plan.

Arrange the drop-off

Steph will set a time for approved pieces. We don't provide in-house pickup, but we're happy to recommend a trusted mover. Keep a note of what was delivered and whose consignor account will receive payouts.

Prepare the Photo Review

A Few Details That Help

You don't need to build a formal inventory for us. Share what you know, and we'll guide the next step.

  • A full photo of each item, plus useful details for smaller groups
  • Visible wear, damage, stains, odors, repairs, or missing parts
  • Maker or retailer labels when they are available
  • Dimensions for larger furniture and rugs
  • Quantity, especially for dining sets or matched groups
  • The date the property must be clear and the best person to contact

Protect the estate before transport

Check drawers, cabinets, cushions, frames, books, and boxes for documents, jewelry, cash, photographs, medication, keys, and personal items. Confirm that components and hardware stay with the correct piece.

If an item may have unusual historic, artistic, or collectible significance, seek the appropriate specialist before consigning it as ordinary home furnishings.

Set Expectations Early

Terms, Timing, and Records

The estate's deadline and our consignment term may be different. A little planning keeps both on track.

Sale and markdown timing

We display approved items for up to four months. Items priced over $60 are marked down 15% every 30 days, helping them reach new shoppers over time.

Estate payout records

The consignor receives 45% of the final sale price. Payouts are ready on the 5th of the following month and picked up in store: under $200 in cash and $200 or more by check.

Unsold pieces

At the end of four months, an item may be renewed or picked up. Items not renewed or collected are released to us and are no longer eligible for pickup or payout.

Use the estate's own professional guidance

We provide consignment service rather than legal, tax, fiduciary, or appraisal advice. Executors and trustees should follow their estate documents and professional guidance for those decisions.

One Part of a Larger Plan

When to Use Consignment, an Estate Sale, or Another Route

A whole household often calls for several channels working together.

Consignment with us

Useful for approved furniture and decor that can come to our showroom and have time to meet the right shopper.

Professionally managed estate sale

May be more appropriate when a substantial share of the household needs to be sold together at the property on a coordinated schedule.

Auctioneer, appraiser, or dealer

Consider specialist help for collections, antiques, fine art, jewelry, rare objects, or items where formal valuation or a specialized buyer pool matters.

Donation, recycling, or clean-out

These routes can address usable everyday items, low-demand furnishings, and remaining materials that do not suit a resale channel or the available timeline.

For a broader comparison, read consignment vs. estate sales and DIY selling. For relocation deadlines, use the moving and downsizing guide.
