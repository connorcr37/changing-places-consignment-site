For Estates and Families

A Respectful, Managed Path for Estate Furniture

When a household carries memories, responsibilities, and a deadline, selective consignment can give appropriate furniture and decor a staffed showroom setting without asking a family member or fiduciary to manage every buyer.

A Selective Channel

Where Consignment Fits in Estate Work

Changing Places can manage the shopper-facing sale of approved furniture and home decor as one focused part of a broader estate plan, alongside any inventory, appraisal, donation, sale, or clean-out work the household needs.

For executors and trustees

Consignment can be one documented route for appropriate furnishings after authority, family distribution, and any required valuation questions have been addressed.

For inheritors and families

A photo-first review lets families identify possible consignment pieces before arranging transport, reducing unnecessary moves during an already demanding process.

For attorneys and estate professionals

Changing Places can serve as a practical referral option when a client has clean, quality furniture or decor and needs a managed showroom channel rather than a private listing.

A Clear Sequence

From Estate Triage to Consignor Payout

Clarify who can make decisions before items leave the property, then document each step in a way that fits the estate's own recordkeeping needs.

Confirm authority and family decisions

Before offering an item, confirm that the person acting for the estate is authorized to do so and that beneficiary distributions or holds have been addressed. Changing Places does not determine ownership or legal authority.

Separate keepsakes and specialist items

Remove personal documents, photographs, heirlooms, and items needing formal appraisal or specialty expertise. Consignment evaluation is based on resale fit, not estate or tax valuation.

Send a focused photo group

Photograph the furniture, rugs, mirrors, wall art, and home decor that appear clean and sale ready. Include condition details, maker labels, dimensions, quantity, and the property's timeline.

Confirm item-by-item fit

The review identifies pieces that suit current demand, condition standards, category needs, and showroom capacity. Keep other appropriate routes in the plan for the remainder of the property.

Arrange delivery and keep records

Changing Places does not provide in-house pickup, though a trusted mover may be recommended. Record what was delivered, the consignor account used, the term, and who will collect payouts.

Prepare the Photo Review

Information That Helps With Estate Triage

A concise, organized submission makes it easier to evaluate groups of items without assuming that every piece belongs in the same channel.

  • A full photo of each item, plus useful detail views
  • Visible wear, damage, stains, odors, repairs, or missing parts
  • Maker or retailer labels when they are available
  • Dimensions for larger furniture and rugs
  • Quantity, especially for dining sets or matched groups
  • The date the property must be clear and the best decision-maker contact

Protect the estate before transport

Check drawers, cabinets, cushions, frames, books, and boxes for documents, jewelry, cash, photographs, medication, keys, and personal items. Confirm that components and hardware stay with the correct piece.

If an item may have unusual historic, artistic, or collectible significance, seek the appropriate specialist before consigning it as ordinary home furnishings.

Set Expectations Early

Terms, Timing, and Records

The estate's deadline and the store's consignment term are different timelines. Plan for both.

Sale and markdown timing

Approved items are displayed for up to four months. Items priced over $60 are marked down 15% every 30 days. A piece may sell quickly, slowly, or not at all.

Estate payout records

The consignor receives 45% of the final sale price. Payouts are issued on the 5th of the following month and picked up in store: under $200 in cash and $200 or more by check.

Unsold pieces

At the end of four months, an item may be renewed or picked up. Items not renewed or collected are released to the store and are no longer eligible for pickup or payout.

Use the estate's own professional guidance

Changing Places provides consignment service, not legal, tax, fiduciary, or appraisal advice. Executors and trustees should follow the governing documents and guidance from their attorney, accountant, appraiser, or other adviser for authority, valuation, reporting, and distributions.

One Part of a Larger Plan

When to Use Consignment, an Estate Sale, or Another Route

A whole household often calls for several channels working together.

Changing Places consignment

Useful for approved furniture and decor that can be delivered to the store and allowed time to sell in a staffed showroom.

Professionally managed estate sale

May be more appropriate when a substantial share of the household needs to be sold together at the property on a coordinated schedule.

Auctioneer, appraiser, or dealer

Consider specialist help for collections, antiques, fine art, jewelry, rare objects, or items where formal valuation or a specialized buyer pool matters.

Donation, recycling, or clean-out

These routes can address usable everyday items, low-demand furnishings, and remaining materials that do not suit a resale channel or the available timeline.
